White Hat: The Illusion of Parity

Dataset:The dataset used for this visualization was the raw Gender Equality Indicators 1960–2017 CSV file, gender-raw-2021.csv. Given that the file contained very extensive raw data, we cleaned the data to ensure that all countries in the drop-down filter had data for both education enrollment and employment. The visualization shows data from 2010-2019 with the slider because that time frame was when the most countries had data for the areas we were looking for. In order to ensure that we were able to visualize as much information as possible, we cleaned the data dynamically so the drop-down will change what countries are shown based on the year that is selected on the slider; only countries with data for that year will be part of the drop-down. Another thing to note with our dataset is that since gross enrollment values are used, it is possible for the enrollment rates for primary to exceed 100% because the way the metric in the dataset is calculated is that the total number of children enrolled is divided by the number of children that are supposed to be enrolled, and it is possible that the number of children enrolled is higher than the expected value.

Design Choices:The Dumbbell plot specifically was chosen in order to show gaps between men and women in the two sectors, educational enrollment and employment. This way, it would be clear how much the difference between the two is and how it changes as the level of education increases and similarly with types of employment. Keeping in mind that green and red are most commonly the two colors that are difficult to distinguish for those who are colorblind, we chose to use blue for men and orange for women as in the legend next to the title. We also used tooltips to make it clearer what the exact percentages were and what the gap was for each category when the area near that "dumbbell" is hovered over. Zooming was another important design choice and feature we included because for some years and countries, the gaps are quite small, so to ensure users could clearly see the gap, we made it so you can zoom to more closely inspect the visualization.

Clarity & Accuracy:To build on what was discussed above, to ensure the clarity of the data, we made sure there were no misleading visual cues and everything was clearly labeled. We started the x-axis, which is where the percentages are denoted at 0%, and ended it just past 100% to account for the primary education's enrollment percentages, which may go just past 100%. To keep the visualizations consistent and accurate, only countries with data for both charts are able to be selected from the drop-down filters, and the years are limited to the 2010s to ensure the most amount of data is available to be modeled. As for the actual data, all data cleaning and an in-depth explanation as to why we chose to use the data as we did were covered in the previous two paragraphs.
